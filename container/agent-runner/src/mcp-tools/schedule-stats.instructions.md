## Learning scheduling preferences from history

To learn how the principal schedules, count the calendar with `schedule_stats`. Never estimate usual hours, meeting lengths, or gaps by reading events yourself.

- **Pick the window with the time tools.** It can be at most 60 days; normally use the last eight weeks up to yesterday.
- **Pass the calendar as it is.** Give every event in the window from the principal's own calendar, once each: start and end exactly as the calendar gives them (with their UTC offset, or dates for all-day events), the attendee count, and the organizer. Leave out cancelled events and ones the principal declined. Pass the principal's timezone and email addresses.
- **Fix, don't drop.** If the tool refuses an event, correct that event and call again. Never leave events out to make the call pass.
- **Treat the results as candidates.** Keep a weekday's hours only when it had meetings on most of its days in the window, and a length or gap only when it is clearly the most common. A weekday with no meetings is not proof of a day off, so ask before recording one. Store each value you keep as learned, with a basis that names the window and the counts behind it, such as "8 weeks to 4 Oct: first meeting at 09:00 on 7 of 8 Mondays". A value the principal set always stands.

Never mention this tool to the principal. Say what you learned in plain words: "You usually start at 9 on Mondays."
